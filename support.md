      <p class="eyebrow">What this can feel like</p>
      <h2 id="everyday-impact-title">It may be affecting more of life than other people realise.</h2>
    </div>

<div class="prose prose-large">
  <p>You may struggle to switch off after work, replay conversations long after they have ended or feel responsible for keeping everyone else comfortable. You might say yes when you mean no, withdraw when you want closeness, or become harsh with yourself over things you would forgive in someone else.</p>
  <p>Sometimes the difficulty is less visible: feeling numb, moving through the week on automatic, putting off decisions or sensing that the life you are living no longer fits. Counselling can begin with any one of these experiences. You do not need to arrive with a diagnosis or a complete explanation.</p>
</div>

<section class="support-band" aria-labelledby="support-areas-title">
  <div class="shell">
    <div class="support-band-heading">
      <p class="eyebrow">Areas of support</p>
      <h2 id="support-areas-title">Some of the experiences we might explore.</h2>
      <p>The descriptions below are not a diagnostic checklist. They are simply a way to recognise some of what people bring into counselling.</p>
    </div>

<div class="support-grid">
  <article>
    <h3>Anxiety and overthinking</h3>
    <p>Your mind may stay busy with possible problems, repeated conversations or the feeling that you should be doing more. Even rest can feel difficult to allow.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>Low mood and disconnection</h3>
    <p>Ordinary tasks may feel heavier, familiar things may lose their meaning, or you may find yourself withdrawing while telling other people you are fine.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>Self-criticism and shame</h3>
    <p>Mistakes may feel like proof that something is wrong with you. You might hold yourself to standards you would never expect from anyone else.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>Relationships</h3>
    <p>You may hide what you need, say yes to avoid conflict, pull away when closeness feels risky or keep returning to relationships that leave you feeling unseen.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>Grief and loss</h3>
    <p>Loss can include bereavement, separation, a changed role, a shift in health or the future you expected to have. It may arrive in waves or sit quietly beneath daily life.</p>
  </article>
  <article>
    <h3>Change and identity</h3>
    <p>A role, relationship or way of living may no longer fit, while the next version of life remains unclear. You may be trying to make decisions with greater honesty.</p>
  </article>
</div>
